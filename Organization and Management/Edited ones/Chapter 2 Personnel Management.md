# Introduction to Personnel Management

## Definition and Concept

**Personnel Management** is the discipline of hiring, developing, and effectively utilizing human resources to make them more valuable to the organization.

**Key Aspects:**

- Effective control and use of manpower
- Obtaining, using, and maintaining a satisfied workforce
- Concerned with the welfare and well-being of employees
- Often referred to as **Human Resource Management (HRM)**
  **Scope of Personnel Management:**
- Company policies and procedures
- Staffing, hiring, and firing
- Orientation and induction
- Wage determination and compensation
- Leave and vacation approval
- Healthcare programs
- Training and performance appraisals

---

## Three Aspects of Personnel Management

| Aspect                          | Focus Area                                 | Key Activities                                                                     |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Welfare Aspect**              | Working conditions and employee well-being | Canteens, housing, personal problems of workers, therapy, health and safety        |
| **Labor/Personnel Aspect**      | Employee management and development        | Recruitment, placement, remuneration, promotions, incentives                       |
| **Industrial Relations Aspect** | Labor relations and dispute resolution     | Trade union negotiations, settlement of industrial disputes, collective bargaining |

---

## Workplace Ergonomics

**Definition:** The science of designing the workplace to fit the worker, reducing strain, fatigue, and injuries.

**Benefits of Proper Ergonomics:**

- Reduces health expenses and work-related injuries
- Improves workplace arrangements and efficiency
- Decreases fatigue and physical stress
- Improves employee comfort and satisfaction
- Contributes to **10-15% productivity improvement** (as per research studies)

**Ergonomic Considerations:**

| Element                  | Considerations                                                        |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Physical Environment** | Positioning and quality of chair, working equipment, protective gears |
| **Movement**             | Heavy lifting techniques, appropriate movements during long shifts    |
| **Lighting**             | Proper lighting conditions                                            |
| **Mental Well-being**    | Stress management, mental workload                                    |
| **Safety**               | Proper application of safety protocols                                |

# Personnel Policy (Employee Handbook)

## Definition and Purpose

**Personnel Policy** (also called **Employee Handbook**) is a statement/document that explains:

- What the employer expects from its employees
- What employees may expect from the employer
  **Distribution:** Must be distributed to each employee at hiring and placement; should be reviewed and discussed during orientation and training sessions.

---

## Contents of a Personnel Policy/Employee Handbook

| Section                    | Content                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Welcome Section**        | Welcome statement from top management, brief history of organization         |
| **Company Values**         | Company's values, morals, objectives, and goals                              |
| **Organization Structure** | Organizational hierarchy and reporting relationships                         |
| **Workplace Rules**        | Hours of work, attendance, pay, benefits, supervision, discipline, attire    |
| **Hiring Policies**        | Recruitment, probationary periods, placement procedures                      |
| **Dismissal Policies**     | Layoffs, termination, disciplinary procedures                                |
| **Leave Policies**         | Sick leaves, holidays, paid leaves, vacations, allowances                    |
| **Compensation**           | Pay structures, benefits, incentives, bonuses, insurance plans               |
| **Legal Policies**         | Harassment policy, work-related accidents, disabilities, security, conflicts |
| **Employee Benefits**      | Dental plans, family benefits, educational assistance, union information     |

**Importance of Discussing Personnel Policy with New Hires:**

- Sets clear expectations from the beginning
- Reduces misunderstandings and conflicts
- Ensures legal compliance
- Builds trust and transparency
- Provides reference for future disputes
- Orients employees to organizational culture

---

## Why Personnel Policy is Important

1. **Clarity:** Provides clear guidelines for employee behavior and expectations
2. **Consistency:** Ensures uniform treatment of all employees
3. **Legal Protection:** Protects organization from legal disputes
4. **Communication:** Communicates organizational values and culture
5. **Reference:** Provides reference for resolving conflicts and issues
6. **Employee Security:** Makes employees aware of their rights and benefits

# Manpower Planning (Human Resource Planning)

## Definition

**Manpower** refers to the actual people working in an organization; the sum total of skills, talents, knowledge, qualifications, creativity, ability, and values of the workforce.

**Manpower Planning** is the process of putting:

- **Right number** of individuals
- **Right kind** of individuals
- At the **right place**
- At the **right time**
- Doing the **right job** they are suitable for

---

## Manpower Planning Procedure

| Step                                        | Description                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Analyzing Current Manpower Inventory** | Assess existing workforce across different departments, skills, qualifications                                             |
| **2. Making Future Manpower Forecasts**     | Analyze future needs based on workloads, prospects, budget, expert requirements                                            |
| **3. Developing Employment Programs**       | Create recruitment, hiring, selection, and placement programs                                                              |
| **4. Designing Training Programs**          | Develop training and development plans for new hires and existing employees to improve skills, capabilities, and knowledge |

---

## Importance of Manpower Planning

| Importance                       | Description                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Higher Productivity**          | Optimal utilization of resources; ensures sufficient information about skills and qualifications |
| **Avoiding Overload/Deficiency** | Ensures organization has required number of manpower; no overloads or deficiencies               |
| **Cost Reduction**               | Reduces excess labor or staff; helps reduce operating costs                                      |
| **Attracting Talent**            | Helps attract prospective employees, new talents, and skills                                     |
| **Motivation**                   | Supports educational and training programs, incentive and benefit plans                          |
| **Succession Planning**          | Ensures availability of talent for future needs                                                  |
| **Career Development**           | Provides opportunities for employee growth and advancement                                       |

# Recruitment and Selection

## Definitions

- **Recruitment:** The process of searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply for jobs in the organization.
- **Selection:** The process of choosing the most suitable candidates from those who have applied.
- **Scientific Selection of Manpower:** A systematic, objective process for selecting employees based on job requirements and candidate qualifications.

**Key Concepts:**

- **Job Analysis:** Determines the requirements and qualifications needed
- **Job Description:** Describes duties and responsibilities
- **Job Specification:** Lists qualifications and skills required

---

## Steps of Recruitment and Selection Process

| Step                              | Description                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Job Analysis**               | Analyze job requirements, create job description and specification                                              |
| **2. Job Forecast/Advertisement** | Advertise vacancy with job title, location, period, functions, contacts through newspapers, websites, pamphlets |
| **3. Application Collection**     | Collect applications from potential candidates                                                                  |
| **4. Reference Check**            | Verify past work experience, work behaviors, educational backgrounds                                            |
| **5. Screening/Shortlisting**     | Filter applications based on minimum qualifications                                                             |
| **6. Written Tests**              | Assess knowledge and skills related to job requirements                                                         |
| **7. Preliminary Interview**      | HR personnel interview to test attitude, knowledge, and confidence                                              |
| **8. Department Interview**       | Department heads assess knowledge, experience, and skills                                                       |
| **9. Physical and Mental Tests**  | Medical examination, drug tests, disability tests, background checks                                            |
| **10. Selection and Appointment** | Select applicant and issue offer letter                                                                         |
| **11. Induction/Orientation**     | Welcome process; introduce organization policies, practices, and purpose                                        |
| **12. Placement**                 | Assign employee to specific job and responsibilities                                                            |

## Interviewing Process

**Definition:** An interview is an attempt to secure maximum information from the candidate concerning their suitability for the job.

**Types of Interviews:**

| Type                      | Description                                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Preliminary Interview** | Initial screening to eliminate unfit candidates         |
| **Panel Interview**       | Multiple interviewers assess the candidate              |
| **Technical Interview**   | Assess specific technical knowledge and skills          |
| **HR Interview**          | Assess attitude, communication, and organizational fit  |
| **Stress Interview**      | Test candidate's ability to handle pressure             |
| **Behavioral Interview**  | Assess past behavior as predictor of future performance |

---

## Referral Approach for Manpower Recruitment

**Definition:** Using current employees to refer potential candidates for job openings.

**Advantages:**

| Advantage              | Description                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Quality Candidates** | Referrals often bring quality candidates                    |
| **Faster Hiring**      | Reduces time-to-hire                                        |
| **Lower Cost**         | Less expensive than external recruitment                    |
| **Better Fit**         | Referred candidates often fit organizational culture better |
| **Higher Retention**   | Referred employees tend to stay longer                      |

**Disadvantages:**

| Disadvantage          | Description                                           |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nepotism**          | May lead to hiring friends/family regardless of merit |
| **Limited Diversity** | May result in homogenous workforce                    |
| **Informality**       | May bypass formal hiring procedures                   |
| **Unfair Advantage**  | Referred candidates may get preferential treatment    |

# Job Analysis, Job Evaluation, and Merit Rating

## Job Analysis

**Definition:** The systematic process of describing aspects of a job and specifying the skills and other requirements necessary to perform it.

**Purpose:**

- Determine why the job exists
- Identify who is qualified to do the job
- Understand how and when the job is done
- Determine conditions required for the job
- Identify tools and equipment needed
- Differentiate one job from others

**Key Terms:**

| Term                  | Definition                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Job**               | Collection of tasks, duties, and responsibilities performed by an individual in exchange for pay      |
| **Job Title**         | Specific name for a job position                                                                      |
| **Job Description**   | Statement describing job title, responsibilities, location, working conditions, salary, and benefits  |
| **Job Specification** | Written record of human qualities required (education, skills, qualifications, knowledge, experience) |
| **Job Design**        | Process of altering the nature and structure of a job to increase productivity                        |
| **Job Evaluation**    | Systematic way of determining the value/worth of a job in relation to other jobs in the organization  |

---

## Job Analysis Methods

| Method            | Description                                                                                    | Advantages                                          | Disadvantages                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Questionnaire** | Job holders fill out questionnaire about nature, complexity, skills, stress, strength required | Standardized, covers many aspects                   | May miss non-verbal aspects                   |
| **Interview**     | Job holders describe tasks and duties performed                                                | Allows description of non-observable duties         | Individual may exaggerate duties              |
| **Observation**   | Direct observation of job holders by job analyst                                               | First-hand knowledge; experience working conditions | Individuals may change behavior when observed |

**Fields Using Observation Method:**

- Machine operators
- Construction workers
- Flight attendants
- Police and traffic workers

---

## Job Description

**Definition:** A statement that tells what is to be done, how it is done, and why.

**Purpose:**

- Describes the job, not the job holder
- Provides both organizational information (location, structure, authority)
- Provides functional information (what the position is, what the work is, what is expected)
- Helps identify who is responsible for what

**Components of a Job Description:**

| Component                    | Description                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Location**                 | Division, department, branch                                 |
| **Job Title/Identification** | Specific job title and code                                  |
| **Job Summary**              | Brief description of overall purpose                         |
| **Duties Performed**         | What is to be done, how it is done, purpose behind each duty |
| **Machine/Tools/Materials**  | Equipment or tools used or required                          |
| **Working Conditions**       | Shifts, weekend loads, heavy lifting, hazardous conditions   |
| **Authority**                | Decision-making authority                                    |
| **Reporting**                | Who the employee reports to                                  |

---

## Job Evaluation

**Definition:** Systematic way of determining the value/worth of a job in relation to other jobs in the organization.

**Purpose:**

- Evaluate skills, qualification, experience, and effort required
- Create job hierarchy and pay differentials
- Build rational and acceptable pay structure
- Determine which jobs should get more pay than others

**Key Point:** Job evaluation rates the **job**, not the person.

**Job Evaluation Methods:**

|                              | Description                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ranking Method**           | Jobs ranked from highest to lowest                   |
| **Classification Method**    | Jobs classified into predetermined grades            |
| **Point Method**             | Jobs scored on factors; total points determine grade |
| **Factor Comparison Method** | Jobs compared on key factors                         |

**Job Classes:** When jobs are grouped together, they form job classes. All jobs within a class are treated similarly monetarily.

---

## Merit Rating (Performance Appraisal)

**Definition:** The process of evaluating employees to determine their effectiveness, personality, potential, and contribution to the organization.

**Purpose:**

- Provide feedback to each individual on performance
- Serve as basis for improving or changing working habits
- Provide data for future job assignments and compensation decisions
- Improve organizational efficiency by mobilizing best possible efforts

**Objectives:**

1. Promotions
2. Salary review
3. Planning job rotation
4. Development and training of individuals

---

## Methods of Merit Rating/Performance Appraisal

**Traditional Methods:**

| Method                          | Description                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ranking Method**              | Oldest and simplest; appraiser ranks employees from best to poor                                                                                                                               |
| **Person-to-Person Comparison** | Each employee compared with others in the group individually                                                                                                                                   |
| **Grading Method**              | Categories predetermined (Outstanding, Satisfactory, Unsatisfactory); employees allocated to grades                                                                                            |
| **Graphic Scale Method**        | Employee's quality and quantity of work assessed on a graphic scale considering factors like character, initiatives, leadership, dependability, creativity, ability, attitude, decision making |
| **Free Essay Method**           | Supervisor writes free-form description of employee performance, including character, initiative, capability, quality with supporting examples                                                 |

**Modern Methods:**

| Method                       | Description                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Appraisal by Result/MBO**  | Managers and employees jointly set goals; rewards based on results achieved                                                                             |
| **Assessment Center Method** | Uses social/informal events, tests, assignments to assess competencies in higher responsibilities; evaluates interpersonal skills, planning, capability |
| **360-Degree Feedback**      | Feedback from superiors, peers, subordinates, and self                                                                                                  |
| **Critical Incident Method** | Records critical incidents of performance                                                                                                               |

# Wages, Salary, and Incentives

## Wage and Salary Structure

**Definitions:**

- **Wage:** Monetary remuneration paid for labor services; computed on hourly, daily, weekly basis or per standard output
- **Salary:** Remuneration paid on a weekly or monthly basis
- **Common Application:** Wages in production, maintenance, labor industry; Salary in corporate, administrative, professional workforce

**Components:**

- Basic wage/salary
- Allowances (family allowances, financial support, lunch allowances)
- Holiday pay and overtime pay
- Bonuses and other benefits

**Direct vs. Indirect Compensation:**

| Type                      | Components                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direct Compensation**   | Basic salary/wage, bonus, overtime pay, holiday pay, profit sharing                                                                                      |
| **Indirect Compensation** | Life and health insurance, retirement allowances, pension, home allowance, family allowance, vehicle allowance, health welfare, social security benefits |

---

## Factors Affecting Wage/Salary Structure

| Factor                             | Description                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Organization's Ability to Pay**  | Wages depend on what the organization can afford                                                 |
| **Supply and Demand of Labor**     | Labor market conditions influence pay; higher demand = higher pay                                |
| **Prevailing Market Rate**         | Comparable wage rates between organizations; helps attract skilled people                        |
| **Living Wage**                    | Wages should enable employee to maintain reasonable standard of living for themselves and family |
| **Productivity**                   | Measured in output per man-hour; depends on labor efforts, management, and technology            |
| **Trade Union's Bargaining Power** | Stronger unions can demand higher wages                                                          |
| **Government Regulations**         | Minimum wage laws and labor regulations                                                          |
| **Job Requirements**               | Skills, education, experience needed                                                             |
| **Geographic Location**            | Regional variations in wages                                                                     |
| **Employee Performance**           | Merit-based differentiation                                                                      |

---

## Incentives

**Definition:** Something that motivates an individual to perform certain action.

**Purpose of Incentive Programs:**

- Increase productivity of employees
- Help employees enjoy work
- Motivate employees to perform better

**Types of Incentives:**

| Type                                      | Description                             | Examples                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Financial/Monetary Incentives**         | Monetary rewards                        | Raise in pay, bonuses, rewards, profit sharing                                                                                     |
| **Non-Financial/Non-Monetary Incentives** | Non-monetary rewards                    | Flexibility in time schedules, holidays, friendly environment, social functions, family benefits, educational development programs |
| **Individual Incentives**                 | Based on individual performance         | Promotions, salary increase, improved conditions, recognition, awards                                                              |
| **Group Incentives**                      | Based on team/group performance         | Based on standard output of a group                                                                                                |
| **Profit Sharing**                        | Employees receive percentage of profits | Motivates better performance                                                                                                       |
| **Employee Benefits**                     | Non-monetary benefits                   | Restaurant coupons, game tickets, movie tickets, gift vouchers, concert tickets, paid vacations                                    |

**Advantages of Individual Incentives:**

- Creates "Each Man for Himself" attitude
- Improves efficiency
- Increases job satisfaction

**Advantages of Group Incentives:**

- Effective when individual performance is difficult to measure
- Increases productivity of the group
- Efficient workers help slower workers meet deadlines

# Separation and Exit Process

## Types of Separation

| Type                    | Description                                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Resignation**         | Voluntary leaving with 30 days notice           |
| **Retirement**          | Mandatory retirement at age 58 (Nepal context)  |
| **Redundancy**          | Skill no longer required by organization        |
| **Medically Unfit**     | Inability to perform job due to medical reasons |
| **Disciplinary Action** | Termination due to misconduct                   |
| **Criminal Offence**    | Conviction on criminal charges                  |

---

## Disciplinary Actions and Penalties

**1. Warning (Verbal/Written)**

| Offence                  | Description                                     |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Unauthorized Absence     | Absence from work without approval              |
| Leaving Without Approval | Leaving workplace without supervisor's approval |
| Frequent Lateness        | Coming late frequently without approval         |
| Disobedience             | Not abiding by senior's orders regarding work   |

**2. Deduction of One Day Remuneration**

| Offence                  | Description                                           |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Refusal to Accept Notice | Not accepting employer's letter or punishment notice  |
| Illegal Strike           | Participating in or compelling illegal strike         |
| Causing Loss             | Reducing production or service recklessly             |
| False Documentation      | Trying to take facilities by submitting false details |
| Safety Violation         | Not using security instruments provided               |

**3. Withholding Annual Salary Increment or Promotion for One Year**

| Offence                   | Description                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Unauthorized Property Use | Taking company property outside or allowing unauthorized use |
| Embezzlement              | Financial embezzlement with company transactions             |
| Property Damage           | Destroying company property due to negligence                |
| Abuse of Facilities       | Abusing items, facilities, health, safety of others          |

**4. Dismissal from Service**

| Offence                | Description                                                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Physical Harm          | Causing bodily harm to employer, employee, or others at workplace         |
| Bribery                | Accepting or offering bribe                                               |
| Theft                  | Stealing company property                                                 |
| Financial Embezzlement | Misappropriation of company finances                                      |
| Intentional Damage     | Knowingly causing damage to company property                              |
| Prolonged Absence      | Absence for more than 30 consecutive days without approved leave          |
| Leaking Secrets        | Disclosing special technology, production formula, competitor information |
| Criminal Conviction    | Convicted on a criminal offence                                           |
| False Documentation    | Presenting false documents for appointment                                |
| Substance Abuse        | Consuming drugs or alcoholic drinks                                       |
| Repeat Offences        | Having been punished twice for other misconducts (third strike)           |
| Harassment             | Engaging in harassment                                                    |
